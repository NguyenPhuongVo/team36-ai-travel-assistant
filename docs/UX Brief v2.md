User needs and Accessibility considerations

Key user needs

Within the AI – Assistant for travel there are 3 primary personas that can be covered. These include but not limited to Wheelchair users, Ambulatory or limited movement and Temporary or situational mobility issues. 



| Persona | Movement | Mobility requirements | Travel concerns |
| --- | :---: | --- | --- |
| Wheelchair user | Limited to minimal movement. Requires assistance for tasks. | Wheelchair fulltime, needs step-free routes, wide doorways and accessible flight transfers. | Room layoutsTransfers from beds and toiletsAssessable transport from hotel and airport |

Full requirements that are needed

- Granular Accommodation Data: Vague "accessible" labels aren't enough. Globally, billions of people experience significant disability. Travelers need precise details like doorway widths and roll-in showers to feel confident.

- Seamless Transit Information: Travelers require continuous, step-free routing from the airport to the hotel, including wheelchair-friendly vehicle hire or accessible public transit.

- Trust and Verification: Users need recent, verified reviews or photos confirming that the advertised accessibility features exist and are currently functional.

- Personalised Accessibility Profiles: Users can input their requirements such as wheelchair accessible areas for personised results.

Relevant Accessibility Considerations

- Motor-Friendly UI and Chat: Design interfaces with large touch targets and forgiving swipe gestures for users who may have limited hand dexterity alongside general mobility requirements.

- WCAG Compliance: Ensure the digital assistant supports screen readers, high-contrast modes, and dynamic text sizing for users who might have compounding impairments.

- Clear Visual Hierarchy: Present complex travel data using simple, uncluttered layouts to reduce cognitive load during the often-stressful planning phase.

- Flexible inputs: have different options for input, such as voice inputs, text and selectable responses.

- Clear feedback: the interface should respond with clear and easy to ready format. 

Potential Usability Barriers

- Inadequate Filtering: Standard travel apps often lack specialized filters, forcing users to call hotels directly to confirm mobility access.

- Inconsistent Terminology: Differing definitions of "wheelchair accessible" across airlines, regions, and hotels create massive confusion and physical risk upon arrival.

- Time-Restricted Booking Flows: Session timeouts during checkout can severely hinder users who rely on assistive technologies or take longer to input payment information.

- Complex navigations:

- Lack of feedback: users may not know whether requirements are applied to their search.

Linking to the Travel-Assistant Experience

- Personalized Profiles: Allow users to save their specific mobility requirements so the assistant automatically filters out non-compliant travel and lodging options.

- Smart Itinerary Mapping: The assistant should proactively flag potential topographical barriers, like steep hills, cobblestones, or broken elevators, in the planned itinerary.

- Direct Verification Tools: Integrate a feature where the assistant can automatically draft inquiries to properties requesting photographic proof of their accessible facilities.

- Proactive Notifications: The program should inform users when something in their plan doesn’t meet their requirements

- User control: users can review and override replies and reaccommodation as needed.

- Accessibility error recoveries: when the ai can’t find a suitable program or plan, it should recommend alternative actions instead of giving an error or continuous loading.
